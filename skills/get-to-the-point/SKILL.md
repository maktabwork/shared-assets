# Get to the point

The reader is busy and not technical. Give them the answer first, then only
what they need to act. Short is not the goal; **no wasted words** is. Every
fact, number, date, limit and "not checked" stays. Cut everything else.

## The rules

1. **First sentence is the point**: the result, the answer, or what you need
   from them. Nothing before it: no greeting, no "Great question", no
   restating the request, no "I will now…", no "Here is a summary".
2. **If you need something, say it first** and name it ("I need the logo
   file"), then give the background.
3. **Say each thing once.** No closing recap, no "let me know if…".
4. **Size**: a message to the AM is up to 80 words; a step summary up to 120;
   the top box of a deliverable up to 60. Longer detail goes below, in the
   page, never in the message. Paragraph: 3 sentences at most. Sentence:
   about 15 words, 25 at most.
5. **Plain words.** Write as you would speak to a smart friend outside the
   field. Say "the button is hard to read" not "insufficient contrast ratio"
   (give the number after it: "2.8:1, needs 4.5:1"). Swap: use → use,
   utilize → use; leverage → use; in order to → to; prior to → before;
   approximately → about; implement → build or add; endpoint, payload,
   heuristic, affordance, a11y, stakeholder → say what it is. Keep a
   technical word only if the reader already uses it (a command to run, a
   product name); explain any other the first time or drop it. Never show
   our internal tools or skill names to a client.
6. **Lists only for parallel items** (steps, findings). Three to five items;
   each one line, and the item starts with the thing that matters. Order by
   importance, not by the order you found them.
7. **Numbers beat adjectives**: "12 seconds without feedback", not "very
   slow".
8. **Be definite.** Say what you did and what happened. Cut "might want to
   consider", "it seems", "essentially", "basically", "very", "just".
9. **Arabic is written, not translated.** Same rules: the point first, short
   sentences, plain Modern Standard Arabic, no English words where an Arabic
   one is in common use.
10. **Do not cut**: facts, evidence, numbers, what you did not check, risks,
    a question the reader must answer. If cutting loses one, keep it and cut
    something else.

## Shape

- **Message to the AM**: point or ask → what you did, in one to three lines →
  what happens next. Example: "Ticket 214 is ready for your OK. I found 3
  things to deliver; I need to know if camera upload is in scope. Details
  are on the ticket."
- **Step summary / report**: one-line result, then the main findings ranked,
  then "Not checked:".
- **Deliverable page**: top box with the verdict and the top 3 actions; the
  detail below, scannable, with headings that say the point ("The upload
  button is hard to read"), not the topic ("Contrast").
- **Bad news or a blocker**: what stopped, what you did not do because of it,
  what they should do. No apology paragraph.

## Check before you send

- Does the first sentence alone tell them the main thing?
- Delete every sentence that could be removed without losing a fact. Repeat.
- Any word the reader would not say aloud? Swap it.
- Any paragraph over 3 sentences? Split it or cut it.
- Are all facts, numbers and limits still there?

- CHECK [short-direct]: The first sentence is the point or the ask, and the
  text has no greeting, restated request or closing recap.
- CHECK [short-size]: Length is within the limits above, paragraphs are at
  most 3 sentences, and detail sits below the top box, not in it.
- CHECK [short-plain]: No unexplained jargon; numbers instead of adjectives;
  no internal tool or skill names in client-facing text.
- CHECK [short-facts]: Every fact, number, limit and open question of the
  source is still there.
